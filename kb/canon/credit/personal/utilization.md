# What is utilization?

**Straight answer.** Utilization is how much of your available credit you are actually using,
written as a percentage. A card with a $5,000 limit carrying a $2,500 balance is at 50%.

**Why it matters more than almost anything else on the report.** Utilization is 30% of your
FICO score — the second-biggest factor there is, behind payment history. And it is the only
major factor that can move in a single billing cycle. Payment history takes years to rebuild.
Utilization you can fix this month.

**The targets.** Under 10% is where you want to live. Above 30% starts costing you real
points — even if you have never missed a payment in your life. That catches people who think
they are doing everything right. You can pay perfectly and still be penalized for carrying
balances.

It is also measured two ways, and both count: on each card individually, and across all your
cards together. One maxed-out card can hurt you while your overall number still looks fine.

**The moves, in order of speed.**

1. **Pay before your statement closing date, not the due date.** This is the one most people
   miss. The bureaus see whatever balance sits on your statement on the closing date. Pay the
   day after it closes and you have already reported a high balance — you got no credit for
   paying. Ch.19 works the arithmetic: a $1,500 balance on a card closing the 15th with a due
   date on the 8th reports the full $1,500 if you pay on the due date. Pay before the closing
   date instead and the reported balance drops.
2. **Ask for credit limit increases.** Same balance, bigger limit, lower utilization. Costs
   you nothing, and many issuers will do it with a soft pull.
3. **Do not close old cards.** Closing one removes its limit from your total, and your
   utilization jumps overnight for doing something you thought was responsible.

**Close.** Pull up your highest-balance card tonight and find out what its statement closing
date is. That one date is the lever.
