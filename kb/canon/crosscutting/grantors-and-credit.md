# Do grantors check my credit?

> **Two different questions wear the same word.** "Grantor" means one thing in grant funding
> and another in business credit. Answer the one the member actually asked, and if it is
> ambiguous, answer both — briefly. Getting this backwards is the most likely wrong answer
> in the whole knowledge base.

## If they mean GRANT funders

No. Grant funding has no personal credit check.

That is the whole reason Coach K teaches grants first to people who are mid-repair. From the
Access Granted episode, in her own words: grant funding is *"a debt-free opportunity to grow
your business. There's no personal credit check to get access to the funding. So if you face
personal credit challenges, if you are restoring your personal credit, or if you just don't
want your personal credit impacted, grant funding is the best strategy for you."*

Two honest caveats she gives in the same breath: **grants take time, and grants are not
guaranteed.** So the move is not "grants instead of credit" — it is both, in parallel.

## If they mean BUSINESS CREDIT grantors (vendors and suppliers)

Mostly no, at Tier 1 and Tier 2 — and this is Chapter 6, one of the most useful things in
the Blueprint.

Vendors and suppliers at this stage are not looking at your FICO score, your bankruptcies,
or your charge-offs. They are checking whether your business passes basic legitimacy signals:

- Registered with your state
- An EIN that matches your business identity
- A business phone listed in 411 and public directories
- The same address across every registration
- A real website and a professional business email
- Some operating history — even 30 to 90 days counts

That is a checklist, not a credit score. Which is the point: **your personal credit situation
should not delay your business credit journey.** You repair personal credit in the background
and build business credit through your EIN at the same time. Two tracks, in parallel.

Coach K's own account in Ch.6 is that she has seen clients in the 500s, some with
bankruptcies, approved for real Tier 1 vendor accounts once the business was properly
structured — in some cases $10,000–$25,000 in net-30 within 60 days. **That is her testimony,
not a promise to the member.** Never restate it as what will happen to them.

Tier 3 — actual Visa and Mastercard business products — is different, and often does involve
personal credit or a personal guarantee. Do not imply the whole ladder is credit-free.

## Where the two meet

This is the bridge between the two halves of the community, and it is worth saying out loud
to a member who thinks bad credit disqualifies them from everything: the grant track needs no
credit check at all, and the first two tiers of the business credit track need a legitimate
business, not a good score. Both are open to you today. Personal credit repair runs
underneath them, not in front of them.

## Staleness

Ch.6 names Uline, Quill, and Grainger as Tier 1 examples. Those are illustrative and the
source is not dated — **do not present any vendor as currently offering net-30 or currently
reporting.** The legitimacy checklist is durable; the vendor names are not.
