through Dun & Bradstreet (www.dandb.com) and they will
ask you for some critical information about your business.
         They will ask you for your company address,
business phone number, when you were incorporated,
projected gross sales, how many employees you have, how
much square footage your unit occupies, your company EIN
as well and you are going to have other people ask for your
and many other things. This is why setting up a profile with
DNB gets tricky. When you answer these questions the
wrong way, you won’t get rated high enough to get decent
credit.
         By the way, it may seem like a cheaper alternative to
start a corporation in a state like Nevada (even though you
probably don’t live there) but DON’T do this! I do strongly
recommend that you set something up in your home state.
Do not set something up in Nevada or one of those other
places if you plan on running an actual business there on that
state’s soil.
         Why not get a Nevada corporation if you are in a state
like California where the annual fees are so high? Because
it’s not legal to conduct business this way, that’s why.
