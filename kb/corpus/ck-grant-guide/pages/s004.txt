CHAPTER 3: COMPONENTS OF A GRANT PROPOSAL

3.1 Executive Summary -- provides a concise overview of the grant proposal, highlighting the
key points and objectives of the project. It serves as a summary of the entire proposal and
should capture the reader's attention and interest.

3.2 Problem Statement -- identifies and describes the specific issue or problem the project
aims to address. It provides relevant background information, data, and statistics to
demonstrate the significance and urgency of the problem.

3.3 Goals and Objectives -- goals represent the broad outcomes the project intends to
achieve, while objectives are specific, measurable, attainable, realistic, and time-bound
targets that contribute to the goals. Clear, well-defined goals and objectives guide the
project's implementation and evaluation.

3.4 Project Description -- a detailed explanation of the proposed project, including its
activities, methods, and expected outcomes. It outlines the strategies and approaches
employed to achieve the goals and objectives stated earlier.

3.5 Methodology and Approach -- describes the steps, procedures, and techniques used to
carry out the activities outlined in the Project Description. May include data collection,
analysis, participant recruitment, and other aspects of implementation.

3.6 Evaluation Plan -- outlines the methods and criteria used to assess the effectiveness
and impact of the project. Describes how data will be collected, analyzed, and interpreted.
A well-designed evaluation plan ensures success can be measured and demonstrates
accountability to the grant funder.

3.7 Budget and Financial Information -- a detailed breakdown of the estimated costs
associated with implementing the project, including personnel, equipment, supplies, and
other expenses. May also include potential sources of funding, matching funds, and in-kind
contributions.

3.8 Sustainability Plan -- outlines how the project will continue to have a lasting impact
beyond the grant period. Describes strategies for securing ongoing funding, partnerships,
or resources. Demonstrates the project's viability and potential for long-term success.

3.9 Appendices and Supporting Documents -- letters of support, resumes of key personnel,
project timelines, organizational charts, or any other relevant documents that provide
additional evidence of the project's feasibility and value.

Note: the numbering and subheadings in this chapter may vary depending on the specific
grant application guidelines or requirements.
