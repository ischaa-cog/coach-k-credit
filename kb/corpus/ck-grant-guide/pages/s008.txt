GLOSSARY OF GRANT TERMS

Grant: Financial assistance provided by a grantor to a recipient organization or individual
to support a specific project, program, or initiative.
Grantor: The organization or entity that provides the funding for a grant. Also known as the
funding agency, donor, or sponsor.
Recipient: The organization or individual that receives the grant funding to implement the
project or program. Also referred to as the grantee.
Proposal: A written document submitted to a grantor, outlining the project or program to be
funded and requesting financial support.
Funding Opportunity: A specific grant program or initiative offered by a grantor, outlining
the eligibility criteria, application requirements, and funding priorities.
Letter of Intent (LOI): A brief preliminary document submitted to a grantor to express the
intention to apply for a grant. It provides a summary of the proposed project and allows the
grantor to gauge interest and eligibility before the full proposal submission.
Request for Proposals (RFP): A formal document issued by a grantor providing detailed
instructions, guidelines, and evaluation criteria for applicants.
Needs Assessment: A systematic evaluation of the current conditions, issues, and challenges
within a community or target population, used to identify the specific needs a proposed
project will address.
Objectives: Clear and measurable statements outlining the specific outcomes or results to be
achieved through implementation of a project or program.
Budget: A detailed financial plan outlining the estimated costs and expenses associated with
the project, including personnel salaries, supplies, equipment, and other expenditures.
Evaluation: The process of assessing the effectiveness, impact, and success of a
grant-funded project. Involves collecting and analyzing data to determine whether the
desired outcomes and objectives have been achieved.
Sustainability: The ability of a project to continue its operations and deliver its intended
benefits beyond the grant period. Often involves strategies for securing ongoing funding,
building partnerships, and engaging community stakeholders.
In-Kind Contributions: Non-monetary resources, goods, or services provided by the recipient
or other partners towards the project. Examples include volunteer time, donated equipment,
or pro bono services.
Indirect Costs: Administrative or overhead expenses not directly attributable to a specific
project but necessary for the overall operation of the recipient organization. Grantors may
have specific policies regarding inclusion of indirect costs in grant budgets.
Reporting: The process of providing regular updates and documentation to the grantor on the
progress, achievements, and challenges of the funded project. Reports may include financial
statements, narrative summaries, and outcome measurements.
Compliance: Adherence to the grantor's guidelines, rules, and regulations governing the use
of grant funds. Recipients are typically required to provide documentation demonstrating
compliance with the grant terms and conditions.
Grant Agreement: A legally binding contract between the grantor and the recipient outlining
the terms, conditions, and obligations of both parties regarding the grant funding.
Grant Cycle: The period during which the grantor accepts applications, evaluates proposals,
and makes funding decisions. May occur annually, semi-annually, or on another schedule.
Review Panel: A group of experts, professionals, or stakeholders appointed by the grantor to
evaluate proposals.
Funding Priorities: Specific areas or themes identified by the grantor as their focus for
funding. These priorities guide the grantor's decision-making process when selecting
projects for funding.
