CHAPTER 4
                   Reading Your Credit Report
                   Like a Pro

      Most people never read their credit report. That is like
      handing someone your bank card and never checking the
      statement. Errors on credit reports are MORE common than
      you think — studies show 1 in 5 consumers has a material
      error on at least one bureau. Every error that hurts you costs
      you money every single day until you fix it.

      Personal Information Section
      Name, address history, SSN, date of birth, and employment.
      Review carefully. Incorrect names or addresses can mean
      your file is mixed with someone else's — or indicate identity
      theft.


      Account Summary
      High-level overview of total accounts, balances, and
      payment status. Use this as your map before diving into
      individual accounts.


      Account History (Trade Lines)
      The core of your report. Each account: creditor name,
      account type, date opened, credit limit or loan amount,
      current balance, month-by-month payment history, and
      account status (open, closed, derogatory). This is where you
      find most of your disputes.


      Public Records
      Bankruptcies appear here. As of 2018, tax liens and civil
      judgments were removed from credit reports. If you see
      either of these, dispute immediately — they should NOT be
      there.

      Inquiries
      Hard inquiries (from applications) for the past 2 years. If you
      see an inquiry you did not authorize, dispute it using
      Template 2 in Chapter 10.
