CHAPTER 5
                   Your Legal Rights Under the
                   FCRA
       The Fair Credit Reporting Act is a federal law — your legal
       weapon. Congress passed it to protect consumers from
       inaccurate credit reporting. The credit bureaus and
       creditors are LEGALLY REQUIRED to honor these rights. When
       they don't — and they sometimes don't — they can be sued
       in federal court. Know your rights.


       Right to a Free Report
          One free report per bureau per 12 months at
          AnnualCreditReport.com. If you're denied credit, insurance,
          or employment due to your report, you get a free report
          within 60 days.

       Right to Dispute Inaccuracies
          Inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information MUST
          be corrected or deleted within 30 days of your dispute — 45
          days if triggered by your free annual report.

       Right to Know Who Pulled Your Credit
          You can see all hard inquiries on your report and know
          exactly who accessed your information and when.

       Right to Outdated Information Removal
          Most negatives must fall off after 7 years. Bankruptcy
          Chapter 7 falls off after 10 years. Chapter 13 after 7 years.
          Anything past its limit is ILLEGAL to report.

       Right to Direct Dispute with Creditors
          You can dispute directly with the CREDITOR (not just the
          bureau) when they're reporting inaccurate account-level
          information. This is called a direct dispute.

       Right to Sue
          Violations of the FCRA allow you to sue in federal court for
          actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney fees.
          Many consumer protection attorneys take these cases on
          contingency — meaning you pay nothing unless you win.


       The 'Unverifiable' Strategy — This Is How Entire Collections Get Removed:
       When you dispute an item, the bureau contacts the creditor and asks them
       to verify within 30 days. Many debt collectors — especially on older accounts
       — either ignore the request or cannot produce the original documentation. If
       they cannot verify, the item MUST be deleted. This is 100% legal. This is your
       right under federal law. Use it.
