                  Hard Pulls vs. Soft Pulls:
CHAPTER 13
                  What Hits Your Score and
                  What Doesn't
      I cannot tell you how many people I've met who were afraid
      to check their own credit — terrified they'd hurt their score.
      Let me put that fear to rest right now. Checking your own
      credit NEVER hurts your score. That is a soft pull. What hurts
      your score is applying for new credit — that is a hard pull.

      Here is exactly how they work:

      Hard Inquiries (Hard Pulls) — These Affect Your Score

      A hard inquiry occurs when a lender or creditor pulls your
      credit report to make a lending decision because you
      applied for credit. Hard inquiries are what the bureaus call "a
      sign of new risk."

      The logic is simple:
      If you're applying for a lot of credit at once, you might be in
      financial trouble. Each hard pull typically drops your score by
      5–10 points temporarily.

      Hard Pulls Are Generated By:


        • Applying for a credit card (personal or business)
        • Applying for a car loan or auto financing
        • Applying for a personal loan or debt consolidation loan
        • Applying for a mortgage or refinance
        • Applying for a student loan (private loans)
        • Some apartment rental applications (varies by landlord)
        • Some utility company applications in new service areas
        • Business credit applications requiring a personal guarantee

          Hard inquiries stay on your report for 2 years but only
          affect your score for 12 months. After 12 months their
          scoring impact is zero.
