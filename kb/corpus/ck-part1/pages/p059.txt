CHAPTER 14
                   Unions & the Institutions
                   That Hold Your Money
     Not all financial institutions are the same. The bank you choose
     to build your relationship with directly affects your access to
     credit, the products available to you, the fees you pay, and your
     long-term funding options.

     I want to give you the full picture so you make the right choices
     for where you bank and how you position yourself for funding.


     Traditional Big Banks (National Banks)

     These are the largest financial institutions in the country — FDIC-
     insured, with thousands of branches, massive product libraries,
     and the tightest credit standards.

     Big banks are where you want to build a RELATIONSHIP —
     especially if you're working toward:

     • SBA Loans
     • Large Lines of Credit
     • Major Business Financing

         Chase (JPMorgan) — Excellent for business banking. Chase
         Ink cards are among the best business credit cards
         available. Strong SBA lender.

         Bank of America — Business Advantage checking, strong
         online tools, and a top SBA 7(a) lender nationally.

         Wells Fargo — Large business lending portfolio. Good for
         businesses needing equipment financing and commercial
         credit.

         Citibank — Strong for international                      businesses        and
         premium personal banking products.

         US Bank — Strong Midwest presence, excellent business
         credit card products (Triple Cash card).
