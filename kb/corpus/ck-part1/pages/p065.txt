      What Is the CFPB?
      The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) is a U.S.
      government agency created in 2011 under the Dodd-Frank
      Wall Street Reform Act. Its entire purpose is to protect
      consumers from unfair, deceptive, or abusive practices by
      financial companies.

      Think of the CFPB as your financial watchdog — except it has
      teeth.

      What the CFPB Does
      The CFPB supervises banks, credit unions, credit bureaus,
      debt collectors, payday lenders, mortgage servicers, and
      other financial companies. It writes rules, enforces federal
      consumer financial laws, and takes action against
      companies that violate those laws.


      Your Most Important CFPB Tool — The Complaint Portal
      If a credit bureau ignores your dispute, a debt collector
      violates the FDCPA, or a lender engages in deceptive
      practices, you can file a formal complaint at
      consumerfinance.gov/complaint. Companies are REQUIRED
      to respond to CFPB complaints. Filing one often moves your
      issue faster than any other escalation. This is Letter 11 from
      Chapter 10 put into digital action.

      CFPB vs. FTC
      Both agencies protect consumers — but they serve different
      purposes.
      • CFPB — Credit bureaus, lenders, financial companies
      • FTC — Identity theft and fraud cases
      For identity theft, go to: identitytheft.gov

      Use as both are powerful.
