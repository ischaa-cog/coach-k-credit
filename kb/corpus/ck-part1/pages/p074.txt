IdentityTheft.gov — Your First Stop

IdentityTheft.gov is the federal government’s official resource
for identity theft victims. It is operated by the Federal Trade
Commission (FTC) and should be your first step if your identity
is compromised.

Do not search randomly online—go directly to the official
source.

What IdentityTheft.gov Does for You

   Creates your personalized recovery plan
   Generates a pre-filled FTC Identity Theft Report
   Provides pre-written dispute letters
   Tracks your recovery progress in one dashboard
   Guides you through fraud alerts and credit freezes
   Helps dispute fraudulent accounts and inquiries
   Connects you to additional recovery resources

All services are FREE.

Website: identitytheft.gov - Go here first. It is official. It generates
the documents you need.


The 7-Step Identity Theft Recovery Plan


   Step 1: Go to IdentityTheft.gov Immediately

   Visit the site and follow the prompts.
   Download and save your FTC Identity Theft Report—you’ll
   need it for disputes and creditor communication.

   Step 2: Place a Fraud Alert (One Bureau Covers All)

   Contact any one of the three major credit bureaus.
   They are legally required to notify the others.
      Initial Fraud Alert: lasts 1 year
      Extended Fraud Alert: lasts 7 years (for confirmed
      victims)
