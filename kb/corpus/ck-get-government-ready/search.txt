GET GOVERNMENT READY — SAM.gov REGISTRATION CHECKLIST (Coach K) This download is designed to help you prepare for registration with sam.gov and your local governments. Why SAM registration matters: by being registered with SAM you unlock opportunities to secure government contracts. Once registered with SAM.gov -- and only then -- you are able to register with DIBBS and Unison Marketplace. Having access to these three government websites to discover and bid on a range of contracts, from small to massive, opens up a vast arena of opportunities. [SOURCE CAVEAT FOR THE KB: this handout was written for the courier / medical-courier industry and some of its advice is industry-specific (notably the NAICS naming tip). The registration sequence itself is general and applies to any business. Two dated items: it recommends googledomains.com and self-corrects "(It's now squarespace)", and it promotes a June 4 More Money Summit that has passed. Do not present the event as upcoming.] THE CHECKLIST 1. Choose a name and buy the domain. (Source says Google Domains, self-corrected to Squarespace -- Google Domains was acquired by Squarespace.) 2. Register your entity. Identify the legal structure of your entity (corporation, sole proprietorship, partnership) and provide relevant documentation. Start at your city website. 3. Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Obtain your Employer Identification Number (EIN) or Social Security Number, depending on the type of entity, at irs.gov. 4. Get a DUNS number at dnb.com. 5. NAICS codes. Determine the appropriate North American Industry Classification System codes that best represent your entity's primary business activities. Look them up at naics.com/search. (Industry-specific tip from the source: for courier work, avoid including "Courier", "Transportation", or "Logistics" in your business name.) 6. Business contact information: - Business address -- absolutely no virtual address or PO box. - Business phone number (source suggests phone.com or vonage.com). - Business email address on your own domain (source suggests Google Workspace). 7. Financial information. Prepare annual revenue, number of employees, executive compensation details, established date, and ownership split (e.g. 51/49, 100%) if applicable. 8. Point of Contact details. Designate primary and backup points of contact who will handle SAM.gov updates and notifications. 9. Business bank account. Gather banking details including routing number and account number for electronic funds transfer (EFT). 10. Representations and Certifications. Ensure compliance with federal regulations such as equal opportunity and conflict of interest requirements. 11. Register on SAM.gov (sam.gov/content/home). 12. Register for DIBBS -- the DLA Bidding Board System (dibbs.bsm.dla.mil). Once gathered, input everything into a spreadsheet to keep track of all your important information.
