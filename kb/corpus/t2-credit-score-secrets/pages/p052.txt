


        Here's the stuff that's usually worth the effort of correcting with the bureaus:

                 Late payments, charge-offs, collections or other negative items that aren't
                 yours.
                 Credit limits reported as lower than they actually are.
                 Accounts listed as "settled," "paid derogatory," "paid charge-off" or
                 anything other than "current" or "paid as agreed" if you paid on time and
                 in full.
                 Accounts that are still listed as unpaid that was included in a bankruptcy.
                 Negative items older than seven years (10 in the case of bankruptcy) that
                 should have automatically fallen off your report.


        You actually have to be a bit careful with this last one, because sometimes scores
        actually go down when bad items fall off your report. It's a quirk in the FICO
        credit-scoring software, and the potential effect of eliminating old negative items
        is difficult to predict in advance.

        Some of the stuff that you typically shouldn't worry about includes:


                 Various misspellings of your name.
                 Outdated or incorrect address information.
                 An old employer listed as current.
                 Most inquiries.


        If the misspelled name or incorrect address is because of identity theft or
        because your file has been mixed with someone else's, that should be obvious
        when you look at your accounts. You'll see delinquencies or accounts that aren't
        yours and should report that immediately.
